What we are

Vision. Real-world assets on Arc, for anyone with a wallet. Arc is the first L1 built for stablecoin finance: USDC pays the gas, finality is deterministic and the markets never close. A NEW ONE is the front door to that: one place where a tokenized fund, a credit pool, a stock or a yield vault is launched, found and bought, settled in USDC.

Mission. To be the launch and distribution front end for real-world assets on Arc. The issuer is always a regulated entity, and we never issue anything ourselves: Centrifuge rails carry funds and credit, licensed issuers bring stocks, Morpho and Aave carry the lending. We build the interface: wallet onboarding, USDC settlement, bridging from other chains, and a page for every asset that shows who stands behind it, on what terms, with what on-chain data.

Three rules hold everywhere on the site: we never custody (every deposit, loan and trade is signed by your wallet and lives in a public contract), we never issue (assets come from regulated issuers or open protocols, never from us), and we never advise (we show data and terms, and the choice is yours).

Earn

Earn lists lending vaults on Arc through Circle's Earn service: Morpho vaults where curators lend USDC or EURC into over-collateralised markets. Every card shows the current APY, total deposits, withdrawable liquidity, the vault's performance and management fees, and the curator. You deposit and withdraw from your own wallet; the vault shares (ERC-4626) sit in your address and the balance shown is read from the chain. Vault fees go to the curator, none to us. Yields are variable and a vault marked low liquidity may not let you withdraw at once. A vault can also refuse a deposit by its curator's own caps; the deposit is simulated first, and the page says so before your wallet opens. Vaults holding under 1,000 are shown only under Show unlisted.

Aave V4. Aave's Main market sits first on the page: supply USDC or EURC (cirBTC and WETH too, which earn close to nothing because few borrow them), withdraw any time the hub has the cash. Aave's reserve fee goes to the Aave DAO; supplying and withdrawing cost nothing on our side. Your deposits, at the top of the page, lists everything the connected wallet holds across Morpho and Aave.

Borrow

Borrow lists Morpho markets on Arc through Circle's Borrow Kit: collateral on one side (cirBTC, WETH, sUSDai, PST and more), USDC or EURC on the other. You say how much to borrow; the service sizes the collateral and the quote shows what gets locked, the health factor, the liquidation price and every fee before you sign. A loan opens in a single atomic transaction through the batcher Arc publishes for plain wallets. From the loan card you repay, borrow more, add or withdraw collateral, or close. If the collateral's price falls until the health factor reaches 1.0, Morpho liquidates part of it with a penalty, so keep an eye on the band. On a new loan A NEW ONE takes an origination fee of 0.15% (15 bps), shown in the quote; 90% of it goes to the site and 10% to Arc.

Aave V4. Two Aave markets, each a separate account with its own health factor. Crypto-backed loans (the Main market): USDC or EURC against cirBTC or WETH, the loan follows the crypto price. Currency loans (the Forex market): EURC against USDC or USDC against EURC, up to 90%, the loan follows the EUR/USD rate. Each action (supply collateral, enable it, borrow, repay, withdraw) is one signature, batched on Arc and simulated before your wallet opens; the quote shows the health factor before and after and the liquidation price. The site refuses a borrow or withdrawal that would leave the health factor under 1.15. Repaying more than you owe is safe: Aave takes only the debt. On each amount borrowed on Aave through the site (a first loan or a later draw) A NEW ONE takes an interface fee of 0.15%, sent in the same transaction and shown in the quote; supplying, withdrawing and repaying are free.

Real-world assets

RWA · Funds lists the tokenized funds that Centrifuge brought to Arc on 1 October 2026: the Janus Henderson Treasury Fund (JTRSY, US Treasury bills), the Janus Henderson AAA CLO Fund (JAAA) and the NYLIM US High Yield Bond Fund (HYB), all issued by Anemoy Capital SPC Limited, a BVI professional fund. Each card reads the share price from the vault contract on Arc and the fund size and trailing 30-day yield from Centrifuge's indexer, and names the manager, the fees, the minimum, the liquidity terms and the ratings from the issuer's factsheet, with a link to the executive summary. A fourth card, USYC, is Circle's own tokenized money market fund (see below), so the page lists four funds in all.

The Centrifuge funds are for non-US professional investors, and the issuer decides who qualifies: it runs KYC and marks the wallet as a member of the fund on Arc, after which the share tokens accept transfers to it. Until then the page says the wallet is not whitelisted and sends you to the issuer; nothing on this site can skip that step. A whitelisted wallet places a deposit request in USDC on Arc from the page (the vault router's requestDeposit), the issuer fills orders on its dealing schedule, and the shares are then claimed from the same page. Redemptions work the same way in reverse. Every request carries a message to the fund's hub on Ethereum, and that delivery fee, paid in USDC on Arc with the transaction, is shown in the quote before you sign. A NEW ONE takes no fee on these funds and never custodies anything: USDC goes from your wallet to the fund's escrow, shares come back to your wallet.

USYC is a share of the Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund, a Cayman fund that holds reverse repo on US government securities, run by Circle International Bermuda, and it has been on Arc since launch day. It is for institutions outside the United States, with a 100,000 USD minimum; Circle onboards the investor and whitelists the wallet, and the card stays locked until it does. There is no hub on Ethereum and no message fee: the issuer's Teller mints USYC against your USDC in the same transaction, at the price its oracle sets each business day, and burns it back into USDC at once when you redeem. Price, yield and the Arc supply are read from the chain; the fund's global size comes from the issuer's public price report, relayed by a small function of ours.

Your funds. At the top of the Funds page, a connected wallet sees its shares in each fund valued at NAV, its pending orders and the fills waiting to be claimed, with redeem, claim and deposit one click away.

Stocks from licensed issuers are next. In every case the issuer creates and legally stands behind the asset, runs eligibility and KYC, holds the underlying and handles redemptions; we build the page, the wallet access and the USDC settlement.

Swap & Bridge

Swap & Bridge brings money onto Arc from Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum, OP Mainnet, Polygon, Avalanche, Unichain, Linea, World Chain, Sonic, Monad, Sei, HyperEVM, Ink, Solana and Sui: you pay with what you hold, it lands on Arc as USDC at your address, and you never need gas on Arc to bring it in. USDC crosses on Circle's CCTP, whose Forwarding Service mints it on Arc for you; fast transfers arrive in about 20 seconds where Circle offers them. Anything else (ETH, SOL, a token) is swapped into USDC on the way by LI.FI.

  • From Solana: SOL or any Solana token becomes USDC on Arc in one LI.FI route, signed in Phantom, Solflare or Backpack. The USDC lands at your Arc address: Phantom carries one, or you type it in. On a phone with no Solana wallet, the page offers to open itself inside Phantom or Solflare.
  • From Sui: USDC goes over Circle's CCTP and lands in seconds, signed in Slush, Phantom or Suiet. SUI and other Sui tokens are swapped into USDC first by LI.FI, which makes two signatures. Sui's network fee is paid in SUI; about 0.01 SUI in the wallet is enough.
  • EURC and cirBTC through Circle Gateway: under "EURC · cirBTC" they arrive on Arc as themselves, not as USDC. Deposit EURC (Base, Avalanche, World Chain or Ethereum) or cirBTC (Ethereum) into your Gateway balance; once that chain finalizes the deposit (seconds on Avalanche, about 15 to 20 minutes elsewhere), one signature sends it to Arc and Circle mints it in your wallet there, with no gas needed on Arc.
  • Swap on Arc: the same page swaps USDC for tokens already on Arc (cirBTC, WETH and more), which is how the Borrow tab gets you collateral. The home page carries a small Swap on Arc card that prices a trade from 24h prices and hands it to this page for the exact route, next to a list of Arc's busiest tokens by 24h volume (from GeckoTerminal, limited to tokens the router lists, with $NOAH pinned on top).

Fees: USDC brought as USDC carries only Circle's own transfer fees, shown in the quote. A payment in anything other than USDC carries a 0.25% fee to A NEW ONE, shown as its own line before you sign. On Gateway, Circle's fee (the source chain's gas and the mint on Arc) comes from your Gateway balance; our 0.25% applies only when the extra Gateway gas it causes stays under a fifth of it, so small amounts and most Ethereum balances carry none.

AnewOne.Fun

The launchpad lives on at anewone.xyz/fun, unchanged: a free, permissionless place to launch a coin on a USDC bonding curve that graduates into a locked Uniswap v3 pool. Everything below this line documents it.

The launchpad

AnewOne.Fun is a permissionless memecoin launchpad. Anyone can create a token for free (you pay only network gas), and every token trades immediately on its own constant-product bonding curve priced in USDC. There is no order book, no seeding liquidity, and no listing step. The contract itself is the market maker.

rug-proof

Nothing to pull

Reserves are locked in the curve. At graduation they move into a Uniswap v3 pool, and the position stays inside the platform contract, which has no function that can withdraw it. There is no liquidity a dev, or we, could drain.

USDC-native

One asset

On Arc, USDC pays gas and buys tokens. No volatile gas coin, no priority-fee auctions.

fair launch

Anti-snipe

A per-wallet cap of 2% in the first 20 blocks blunts bots sniping a launch. Per wallet, not per person: it slows a snipe, it does not make one impossible.

verifiable

100% open source

Contracts, frontend and the indexer are all public. Read them, run them, or check any claim on this page against the source: github.com/izzetcakmak/anewone.

The bonding curve

Each token has a fixed supply of 1,000,000,000 (1B), all placed on the curve at creation. The contract has no mint authority. There is no function that can create more, so the supply you see at launch is the supply forever. Price follows a constant-product formula against a virtual USDC reserve, so every buy pushes the price up and every sell pushes it down, smoothly and predictably.

  • FDV = current price × the 1B supply. The floor ranks and labels coins by it, as every launchpad does, but it is a fully diluted value: most of the supply still sits on the curve. The coin page also shows the market cap proper (price × the tokens people actually hold) and the liquidity (the real USDC locked in the curve, or in the Uniswap pool after graduation).
  • Graduation comes at 5,000 USDC raised. Trading on the curve stops there, and the curve moves into a Uniswap v3 pool (1% fee tier, full range) at the price it ended on, so nobody who bought on the curve is marked down. Only as many tokens as the raised USDC pairs with at that price go into the pool; the rest of the unsold supply is burned. Anyone can trigger the move once a coin has graduated, and trading resumes in the pool.
  • Buys and sells always clear against the contract, so there is always liquidity to exit.

Where the supply ends up: a coin that graduates at the 5,000 USDC target settles at roughly 55.6% held by the people who bought it, 24.7% locked in the Uniswap pool, and 19.7% burned. Every curve opens with 4,000 USDC of virtual reserve that sets the starting price and never existed as real money, which is why the raised USDC cannot pair with every unsold token. If it did, the pool would open about 44% below the curve's last price and everyone who bought on the curve would be down the moment it moved. Burning the difference is what keeps the price continuous, and it means the leftover supply ends up with nobody at all - not held back by us.

Fees & creator earnings

Every trade pays a flat 1.5% fee:

SliceGoes toNotes
0.5%Token creatorCollects in a pot; claimable within a 7-day window from the moment the pot starts accruing
1%PlatformSplit evenly between the platform owners as it accrues; each owner withdraws only their own share. Also receives creator pots left unclaimed past the window

How the window works: the 7 days start when your pot goes from empty to non-empty, and every fee you earn while the pot is open joins it under the same deadline. Claiming empties the pot, and the next fee starts a new pot with a fresh 7 days. If a pot is not claimed in time, only that pot goes to the platform (anyone can trigger the sweep); the very next fee you earn starts a fresh pot and a fresh 7 days, so nothing you earn afterwards is affected. A creator who claims before the displayed deadline keeps the full pot. Your creator panel shows the pot and the exact time left on it; the window only catches pots nobody comes back for. The Arc testnet platform predates this schedule and charges 1%, half to the creator.

After graduation the Uniswap pool charges its own 1% fee tier. Its USDC side is split between creator and platform in the same proportion as a curve fee, a third to the creator; its token side is burned.

The $NOAH buyback & burn

$NOAH was the first coin launched on AnewOne.Fun and the first memecoin on Arc's public mainnet. Starting the week after it graduates, half of the platform's 1% fee slice on AnewOne.Fun (see the table above) from every buyback week is used to buy $NOAH in its Uniswap pool and burn it, on the Sunday that closes the week, at 09:00 UTC. Buyback weeks take turns with prize weeks (see the prize race). Its fixed 1B supply can only go down.

  • Onchain: each burn is a public transaction on Arc. The tx link is posted on the board, so anyone can check the amount against the chain.
  • A platform decision, not a contract rule: the launchpad contract does not run the burn. The platform carries it out from its own share of the fees, and it can change or stop, in which case it is announced here first.
  • No promise on price: a smaller supply does not set a price. $NOAH is a memecoin, with no claim on the platform, its revenue or its assets. Nothing here is investment advice.

Community vote, 22–24 Oct 2026

The community voted on how this budget, the Ecosystem Budget, is spent. Final count, 46 votes: Yes 36, No 10 on adding weekly USDC prizes; among the 36 Yes votes, Hybrid 15, Alternating 11, Classic 10. By the rule published with the vote, the program goes ahead. Hybrid had the most votes; the platform runs the program as alternating weeks instead, a prize week and then a buyback week, in turn. As the vote said, this change is announced here before it starts. The final count is also on the vote page. Prizes are a draw, not a return.

The prize race

From Sunday 25 Oct 2026, 09:00 UTC, prize weeks and $NOAH buyback weeks take turns. In a prize week, three traders on AnewOne.Fun win USDC. The pot is 0.5% of that week's trading volume (half of the platform's 1% fee slice, the same rate the buyback uses), paid from the platform's own share of the fees; no fee changes. The first prize week runs to Sunday 1 Nov, 09:00 UTC, the first buyback week to Sunday 8 Nov, the next prize week to Sunday 15 Nov, and so on.

  • The week: Sunday 09:00 UTC to the next Sunday 09:00 UTC. At 09:00 the board resets; when a prize week begins, the race starts again from zero that minute.
  • Volume: every USDC buy and sell on any AnewOne.Fun coin, at the trade's value (what a buyer paid, what a seller got before the fee), read from the platform's Trade events on Arc. The board on AnewOne.Fun updates with every trade, and the published list at least every 15 minutes.
  • Who qualifies: the 10 wallets with the most volume when the prize week closes. Team wallets are left out. No sign-up, no minimum, nothing to hold.
  • The draw: on the Sunday that closes a prize week, at 09:00 UTC, 3 of the 10 are drawn live and the draw is recorded.
  • The payout: each winner gets a third of the pot in USDC, sent to the wallet that traded, within a day of the draw. The winners, the recording and the payout transactions are posted on the board.
  • The buyback week: no race. Its 0.5% goes to the $NOAH buyback on the closing Sunday at 09:00 UTC (once $NOAH has graduated, see above), posted on the board with its transaction.
  • A platform decision, not a contract rule: like the burn, the prize is run by the platform, and any change is announced here first. Prizes are a draw, not a return. Nothing here is investment advice.

Safety by design

  • Rug-proof: curve reserves can never be withdrawn, only traded against, and at graduation they move once into a Uniswap v3 position the contract keeps for good. The Uniswap addresses are fixed in the contract at deploy, so nobody can point a migration anywhere else. Fees are held in separate accounting from reserves.
  • Anti-snipe: for the first 20 blocks after launch, each wallet can buy at most 2% of supply. It is a per-wallet cap: someone with several wallets can take 2% with each, so it slows and spreads a snipe rather than making one impossible. The creator's own dev buy is under the same cap.
  • Verified & open: the contract is public and the launchpad is a static page you can inspect; the few server functions behind Deck Hand, card purchases and bridging are listed on the privacy page. Token names, images and comments are escaped, so a hostile token can't attack you.

Launch a token

  1. Connect a wallet (or continue with email or a social login; see Wallets).
  2. Click + Create Coin.
  3. Set name, symbol, and optionally an image (JPG/PNG, ≤5 MB, ≥100×100, auto-cropped & stored on-chain), a description and socials (X / Telegram / website).
  4. Optionally add a small dev buy at launch (still subject to the anti-snipe cap).
  5. Confirm. You pay only gas. Launching is free. Your token is live and tradable instantly.
Metadata is stored fully on-chain, so your token's image and links can't rot or be swapped out from under holders.

Partner launch links. An app that wants its users to launch here can link to fun/?launch=1&ref=<code>: launch=1 opens the form as soon as a wallet is connected, and a ref code from our short list of partners is written into the coin's own metadata by the launch transaction. Which coins came through a partner is therefore public and permanent on Arc, and a partner's share is computed from the chain, not from a list either side keeps. Nothing changes for the person launching or for the fees they pay. The code is remembered in the browser for 30 days.

Trade

  • Open any token to see its market cap, holders, 24h volume, age and a live price chart.
  • Buy or sell with an amount or the 25 / 50 / 75 / MAX presets.
  • Set a max slippage (1 / 2 / 5% or custom). The contract enforces a minimum-output so a fast-moving curve can't fill you far from the quote.
  • Your position card shows real cost basis and P&L using a live sell quote (fees + curve impact included), not an optimistic price × balance.
  • Discover coins via Trending / Newest / Top FDV / Graduating, search, advanced filters, a live trade feed, and a watchlist.
  • Your coins lists every coin the connected wallet holds, valued at its last price, with trade links and any creator fees waiting to be collected. A coin's page also lists its top holders, largest first, with the curve, the pool and burned coins set apart and the creator and you tagged.

Wallets & funding

Connect

Use any injected wallet (MetaMask, OKX, Rabby) or Continue with Email or Social (Google, X, Discord, Apple or a one-time email code) to instantly create a non-custodial embedded wallet with no seed phrase. Both paths behave identically once connected. On a phone, Safari or Chrome cannot talk to a wallet app directly: pick WalletConnect in the connect dialog and it hands you over to Rabby, Trust, MetaMask mobile or any other wallet app and back (on a desktop it shows a QR code for the phone). The dialog can also open the page inside MetaMask or OKX, or copy the link for a wallet's built-in browser.

Fund it

A fresh wallet needs USDC on Arc (it's the gas and the money). On testnet, the app shows a Get test USDC button that opens Circle's faucet (20 USDC every 2h, pick Arc Testnet). The funding panel disappears automatically once the balance lands.

Arc mainnet is live (since 16 Sep 2026) and the floor runs there, and there are two ways to fund a wallet on it. Buy USDC with a card: the app opens Circle's onramp in place, so you go from a card to USDC on Arc without leaving the page or owning any crypto first. It asks for one wallet signature to start, and a Google sign-in wallet works exactly like an injected one. Or bring what you already hold through Swap & Bridge: pay with ETH, SOL, USDC or almost anything on Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Solana, Sui and more, and it lands on Arc as USDC. The testnet faucet flow above only applies to the testnet build.

Portfolio

anewone.xyz/portfolio is one read-only page for everything a wallet holds on A NEW ONE: the stablecoins in the wallet, Aave V4 and Morpho deposits and loans, shares in the tokenized funds, and AnewOne.Fun coins, each valued in USD and read straight from the chain. Connect a wallet or paste any Arc address to look at it (WalletConnect and email sign-in wallets have an address too). The page only reads: it asks for no signature, and every action opens on the page it belongs to. It also raises alerts, for example when an Aave or Morpho health factor nears 1.0 or when a wallet has no USDC to pay gas. The last address shown stays in your browser only.

Deckhand: the AI assistant that remembers

anewone.xyz/chat is an assistant that answers from the live floor index (every coin, its price, USDC raised and distance to graduation, stamped with the Arc block it read) and explains how launching, trading and Swap & Bridge work. It also knows $NOAH, the Gateway route and the Aave markets. A lighter version of it sits in the corner of every page and answers questions about the whole site without any sign-in and without memory, within a daily allowance per visitor. It cannot trade, sign or move funds. It is an interface to public on-chain data, not an adviser: it will not tell you what to buy, sell or hold and will not predict prices.

Memory on Walrus

Sign in with your wallet by signing one plain message (no transaction, no gas, no Sui wallet) and the deck hand keeps what you tell it across sessions, browsers and devices. Each fact is extracted from your words, Seal-encrypted and stored as its own blob on Walrus mainnet through Walrus Memory (MemWal), under a namespace derived from your address on the server. The delegate key never reaches the browser, and a namespace is never accepted from the request, so one wallet cannot read another's memory.

  • Two chains, two jobs. Coins and trades stay on Arc; only the conversation's memory lives on Walrus. The page shows what was recalled and what was saved on every turn.
  • About a minute to land. A fact saved now is embedded, encrypted and uploaded to Walrus before recall can see it, so it shows up on your next question, not the current one.
  • Only your words are kept. The reply's prices and progress are never written to memory; they are re-read live from Arc on every turn.
  • Open model. Answers come from Qwen3.8 27B on Groq, swappable by configuration. The memory account is a public Sui object, linked from the page, so anyone can verify the blobs exist.

Trust signals

Every token page carries an honest risk read-out so you can judge before you buy:

  • 🔒 Rug-proof is structural, always true, and links the verified contract.
  • Dev holds % is the creator's real balance vs circulating supply (green <5%, amber 5-20%, red ≥20%).
  • Top wallet % and Snipers % show concentration estimated from trades (labelled, since direct transfers aren't counted).
  • Creator profile & reputation score covers a wallet's past launches, graduation rate and whether they exited their own tokens.
  • On-chain comments are a per-token thread; you must hold the token (or be its creator) to post, so it resists comment-bot spam.

Agent API

The index behind the floor is open to programs. /api/basedbot serves it free: every coin with price, caps, liquidity and volume, one coin with its holder concentration and last trades, the 24h trade tape, the distribution and OHLCV candles, rate limited per IP. /api/agent serves the same answers priced per call in USDC under the x402 protocol, settled by Circle's Facilitator Service on Arc mainnet: an agent calls an endpoint, receives a 402 with the accepted options, signs a USDC authorization and retries. The list, a coin, the tape and the distribution cost $0.001 a call, candles $0.002. No wallet is ever named in the data: holders are a count and concentration shares. The full contract is the OpenAPI spec.

FAQ

Is it really rug-proof?

"Rug-proof" means one specific thing here: the liquidity cannot be pulled. Curve reserves can only be traded against, and after graduation the Uniswap position sits in the platform contract, which has no function that withdraws it, so the classic "pull the LP" rug is impossible by construction. It does not mean the price cannot fall: a creator or a large holder can still sell everything they hold, on the curve or in the pool, and the price moves with it like on any market. The trust panel on each coin surfaces exactly that (Dev holds % / Dev exits / top holders), so read it before you buy.

What does it cost to launch?

Nothing but gas. There's no listing fee.

Why USDC for gas?

Arc is Circle's stablecoin L1 where the native gas token is USDC. One stable asset covers fees and trades, with no second coin to acquire.

Do I keep control of my Google wallet?

Yes. It's a non-custodial embedded wallet; neither the platform nor the provider can move your funds. For a large or long-term stash, an injected wallet you fully control is still recommended.
