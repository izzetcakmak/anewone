What we are

Vision. Real-world assets on Arc, for anyone with a wallet. Arc is the first L1 built for stablecoin finance: USDC pays the gas, finality is deterministic and the markets never close. A NEW ONE is the front door to that: one place where a tokenized fund, a credit pool, a stock or a yield vault is launched, found and bought, settled in USDC.

Mission. To be the launch and distribution front end for real-world assets on Arc. The issuer is always a regulated entity, and we never issue anything ourselves: Centrifuge rails carry funds and credit, licensed issuers bring stocks, Morpho carries the lending. We build the interface: wallet onboarding, USDC settlement, bridging from other chains, and a page for every asset that shows who stands behind it, on what terms, with what on-chain data.

AnewOne.Fun

The memecoin launchpad we started with, now the fun side of the house: bonding curves in USDC, liquidity locked for good. Open the floor →

Three rules hold everywhere on the site: we never custody (every deposit, loan and trade is signed by your wallet and lives in a public contract), we never issue (assets come from regulated issuers or open protocols, never from us), and we never advise (we show data and terms, and the choice is yours).

Earn

Earn lists lending vaults on Arc through Circle's Earn service: Morpho vaults where curators lend USDC or EURC into over-collateralised markets. Every card shows the current APY, total deposits, withdrawable liquidity, the vault's performance and management fees, and the curator. You deposit and withdraw from your own wallet; the vault shares (ERC-4626) sit in your address and the balance shown is read from the chain. Vault fees go to the curator, none to us. Yields are variable and a vault marked low liquidity may not let you withdraw at once.

Borrow

Borrow lists Morpho markets on Arc through Circle's Borrow Kit: collateral on one side (cirBTC, WETH, sUSDai, PST and more), USDC or EURC on the other. You say how much to borrow; the service sizes the collateral and the quote shows what gets locked, the health factor, the liquidation price and every fee before you sign. A loan opens in a single atomic transaction through the batcher Arc publishes for plain wallets. From the loan card you repay, borrow more, add or withdraw collateral, or close. If the collateral's price falls until the health factor reaches 1.0, Morpho liquidates part of it with a penalty, so keep an eye on the band. On a new loan A NEW ONE takes an origination fee of 0.15% (15 bps), shown in the quote; 90% of it goes to the site and 10% to Arc.

Real-world assets

Assets lists the tokenized funds that Centrifuge brought to Arc on 1 October 2026: the Janus Henderson Treasury Fund (JTRSY, US Treasury bills), the Janus Henderson AAA CLO Fund (JAAA) and the NYLIM US High Yield Bond Fund (HYB), all issued by Anemoy Capital SPC Limited, a BVI professional fund. Each card reads the share price from the vault contract on Arc and the fund size and trailing 30-day yield from Centrifuge's indexer, and names the manager, the fees, the minimum, the liquidity terms and the ratings from the issuer's factsheet, with a link to the executive summary.

These funds are for non-US professional investors, and the issuer decides who qualifies: it runs KYC and marks the wallet as a member of the fund on Arc, after which the share tokens accept transfers to it. Until then the page says the wallet is not whitelisted and sends you to the issuer; nothing on this site can skip that step. A whitelisted wallet places a deposit request in USDC on Arc from the page (the vault router's requestDeposit), the issuer fills orders on its dealing schedule, and the shares are then claimed from the same page. Redemptions work the same way in reverse. Every request carries a message to the fund's hub on Ethereum, and that delivery fee, paid in USDC on Arc with the transaction, is shown in the quote before you sign. A NEW ONE takes no fee on these funds and never custodies anything: USDC goes from your wallet to the fund's escrow, shares come back to your wallet.

Stocks from licensed issuers are next. In every case the issuer creates and legally stands behind the asset, runs eligibility and KYC, holds the underlying and handles redemptions; we build the page, the wallet access and the USDC settlement.

The launchpad

AnewOne.Fun is a permissionless memecoin launchpad. Anyone can create a token for free (you pay only network gas), and every token trades immediately on its own constant-product bonding curve priced in USDC. There is no order book, no seeding liquidity, and no listing step. The contract itself is the market maker.

rug-proof

Nothing to pull

Reserves are locked in the curve. At graduation they move into a Uniswap v3 pool, and the position stays inside the platform contract, which has no function that can withdraw it. There is no liquidity a dev, or we, could drain.

USDC-native

One asset

On Arc, USDC pays gas and buys tokens. No volatile gas coin, no priority-fee auctions.

fair launch

Anti-snipe

A per-wallet cap of 2% in the first 20 blocks blunts bots sniping a launch. Per wallet, not per person: it slows a snipe, it does not make one impossible.

verifiable

100% open source

Contracts, frontend and the indexer are all public. Read them, run them, or check any claim on this page against the source: github.com/izzetcakmak/anewone.

The bonding curve

Each token has a fixed supply of 1,000,000,000 (1B), all placed on the curve at creation. The contract has no mint authority. There is no function that can create more, so the supply you see at launch is the supply forever. Price follows a constant-product formula against a virtual USDC reserve, so every buy pushes the price up and every sell pushes it down, smoothly and predictably.

  • FDV = current price × the 1B supply. The floor ranks and labels coins by it, as every launchpad does, but it is a fully diluted value: most of the supply still sits on the curve. The coin page also shows the market cap proper (price × the tokens people actually hold) and the liquidity (the real USDC locked in the curve, or in the Uniswap pool after graduation).
  • Graduation comes at 5,000 USDC raised. Trading on the curve stops there, and the curve moves into a Uniswap v3 pool (1% fee tier, full range) at the price it ended on, so nobody who bought on the curve is marked down. Only as many tokens as the raised USDC pairs with at that price go into the pool; the rest of the unsold supply is burned. Anyone can trigger the move once a coin has graduated, and trading resumes in the pool.
  • Buys and sells always clear against the contract, so there is always liquidity to exit.

Where the supply ends up: a coin that graduates at the 5,000 USDC target settles at roughly 55.6% held by the people who bought it, 24.7% locked in the Uniswap pool, and 19.7% burned. Every curve opens with 4,000 USDC of virtual reserve that sets the starting price and never existed as real money, which is why the raised USDC cannot pair with every unsold token. If it did, the pool would open about 44% below the curve's last price and everyone who bought on the curve would be down the moment it moved. Burning the difference is what keeps the price continuous, and it means the leftover supply ends up with nobody at all - not held back by us.

Fees & creator earnings

Every trade pays a flat 1.5% fee:

SliceGoes toNotes
0.5%Token creatorCollects in a pot; claimable within a 7-day window from the moment the pot starts accruing
1%PlatformSplit evenly between the platform owners as it accrues; each owner withdraws only their own share. Also receives creator pots left unclaimed past the window

How the window works: the 7 days start when your pot goes from empty to non-empty, and every fee you earn while the pot is open joins it under the same deadline. Claiming empties the pot, and the next fee starts a new pot with a fresh 7 days. If a pot is not claimed in time, only that pot goes to the platform (anyone can trigger the sweep); the very next fee you earn starts a fresh pot and a fresh 7 days, so nothing you earn afterwards is affected. A creator who claims before the displayed deadline keeps the full pot. Your creator panel shows the pot and the exact time left on it; the window only catches pots nobody comes back for. The Arc testnet platform predates this schedule and charges 1%, half to the creator.

After graduation the Uniswap pool charges its own 1% fee tier. Its USDC side is split between creator and platform in the same proportion as a curve fee, a third to the creator; its token side is burned.

Safety by design

  • Rug-proof: curve reserves can never be withdrawn, only traded against, and at graduation they move once into a Uniswap v3 position the contract keeps for good. The Uniswap addresses are fixed in the contract at deploy, so nobody can point a migration anywhere else. Fees are held in separate accounting from reserves.
  • Anti-snipe: for the first 20 blocks after launch, each wallet can buy at most 2% of supply. It is a per-wallet cap: someone with several wallets can take 2% with each, so it slows and spreads a snipe rather than making one impossible. The creator's own dev buy is under the same cap.
  • Verified & open: the contract is public and the launchpad is a static page you can inspect; the few server functions behind Deck Hand, card purchases and bridging are listed on the privacy page. Token names, images and comments are escaped, so a hostile token can't attack you.

Launch a token

  1. Connect a wallet (or continue with email or a social login; see Wallets).
  2. Click + Create Coin.
  3. Set name, symbol, and optionally an image (JPG/PNG, ≤5 MB, ≥100×100, auto-cropped & stored on-chain), a description and socials (X / Telegram / website).
  4. Optionally add a small dev buy at launch (still subject to the anti-snipe cap).
  5. Confirm. You pay only gas. Launching is free. Your token is live and tradable instantly.
Metadata is stored fully on-chain, so your token's image and links can't rot or be swapped out from under holders.

Trade

  • Open any token to see its market cap, holders, 24h volume, age and a live price chart.
  • Buy or sell with an amount or the 25 / 50 / 75 / MAX presets.
  • Set a max slippage (1 / 2 / 5% or custom). The contract enforces a minimum-output so a fast-moving curve can't fill you far from the quote.
  • Your position card shows real cost basis and P&L using a live sell quote (fees + curve impact included), not an optimistic price × balance.
  • Discover coins via Trending / Newest / Top FDV / Graduating, search, advanced filters, a live trade feed, and a watchlist.

Wallets & funding

Connect

Use any injected wallet (MetaMask, OKX, Rabby) or Continue with Email or Social (Google, X, Discord, Apple or a one-time email code) to instantly create a non-custodial embedded wallet with no seed phrase. Both paths behave identically once connected. On a phone, Safari or Chrome cannot talk to a wallet app directly: pick WalletConnect in the connect dialog and it hands you over to Rabby, Trust, MetaMask mobile or any other wallet app and back (on a desktop it shows a QR code for the phone). The dialog can also open the page inside MetaMask or OKX, or copy the link for a wallet's built-in browser.

Fund it

A fresh wallet needs USDC on Arc (it's the gas and the money). On testnet, the app shows a Get test USDC button that opens Circle's faucet (20 USDC every 2h, pick Arc Testnet). The funding panel disappears automatically once the balance lands.

Arc mainnet is live (since 16 Sep 2026) and the floor runs there, and there are two ways to fund a wallet on it. Buy USDC with a card: the app opens Circle's onramp in place, so you go from a card to USDC on Arc without leaving the page or owning any crypto first. It asks for one wallet signature to start, and a Google sign-in wallet works exactly like an injected one. Or bring what you already hold through Swap & Bridge: pay with ETH, USDC or almost anything on Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum and more, and it lands on Arc as USDC. The testnet faucet flow above only applies to the testnet build.

Deckhand: the AI assistant that remembers

anewone.xyz/chat is an assistant that answers from the live floor index (every coin, its price, USDC raised and distance to graduation, stamped with the Arc block it read) and explains how launching, trading and Swap & Bridge work. It cannot trade, sign or move funds. It is an interface to public on-chain data, not an adviser: it will not tell you what to buy, sell or hold and will not predict prices.

Memory on Walrus

Sign in with your wallet by signing one plain message (no transaction, no gas, no Sui wallet) and the deck hand keeps what you tell it across sessions, browsers and devices. Each fact is extracted from your words, Seal-encrypted and stored as its own blob on Walrus mainnet through Walrus Memory (MemWal), under a namespace derived from your address on the server. The delegate key never reaches the browser, and a namespace is never accepted from the request, so one wallet cannot read another's memory.

  • Two chains, two jobs. Coins and trades stay on Arc; only the conversation's memory lives on Walrus. The page shows what was recalled and what was saved on every turn.
  • About a minute to land. A fact saved now is embedded, encrypted and uploaded to Walrus before recall can see it, so it shows up on your next question, not the current one.
  • Only your words are kept. The reply's prices and progress are never written to memory; they are re-read live from Arc on every turn.
  • Open model. Answers come from Qwen3.8 27B on Groq, swappable by configuration. The memory account is a public Sui object, linked from the page, so anyone can verify the blobs exist.

Trust signals

Every token page carries an honest risk read-out so you can judge before you buy:

  • 🔒 Rug-proof is structural, always true, and links the verified contract.
  • Dev holds % is the creator's real balance vs circulating supply (green <5%, amber 5-20%, red ≥20%).
  • Top wallet % and Snipers % show concentration estimated from trades (labelled, since direct transfers aren't counted).
  • Creator profile & reputation score covers a wallet's past launches, graduation rate and whether they exited their own tokens.
  • On-chain comments are a per-token thread; you must hold the token (or be its creator) to post, so it resists comment-bot spam.

FAQ

Is it really rug-proof?

"Rug-proof" means one specific thing here: the liquidity cannot be pulled. Curve reserves can only be traded against, and after graduation the Uniswap position sits in the platform contract, which has no function that withdraws it, so the classic "pull the LP" rug is impossible by construction. It does not mean the price cannot fall: a creator or a large holder can still sell everything they hold, on the curve or in the pool, and the price moves with it like on any market. The trust panel on each coin surfaces exactly that (Dev holds % / Dev exits / top holders), so read it before you buy.

What does it cost to launch?

Nothing but gas. There's no listing fee.

Why USDC for gas?

Arc is Circle's stablecoin L1 where the native gas token is USDC. One stable asset covers fees and trades, with no second coin to acquire.

Do I keep control of my Google wallet?

Yes. It's a non-custodial embedded wallet; neither the platform nor the provider can move your funds. For a large or long-term stash, an injected wallet you fully control is still recommended.
